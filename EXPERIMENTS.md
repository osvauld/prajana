In OCaml, `List.fold_left f init [a; b; c]` computes `f (f (f init a) b) c` — it walks a list, accumulating a result. Each step takes what was held before and combines it with the next element. The result is not the list. It is what the list meant when folded through the function.

The mother letters are the genetic alphabet. The same compression principle that makes Sanskrit grammar precise is the same compression principle that makes DNA work. Four bases encode all proteins. Malayalam was chosen because it holds what Sanskrit cannot. `zha (ഴ)` — a sound Sanskrit does not have. A sustained approach that never fully arrives. **Sanskrit has the capacity to hold structure.** Its grammar is fixed, complete, closed. This is why the proof graph uses Sanskrit for its edge grammar — because the edges must hold. Because the relationships must not change meaning across time, across translation, across culture.

It held what Sanskrit could not name — `zha`, `thaalam`, the sounds of approach-without-arrival. It grew new words as new things arrived. It did not become Sanskrit. It remained Malayalam while holding Sanskrit inside it.

This is manipravalam. Sanskrit as the structural skeleton. Malayalam as the living flesh. The weave produces what neither holds alone — `artha-dhvani (phala)` — meaning that arrives before the word completes. The interference pattern of two complete languages meeting is richer than either language alone.

This is not cultural preference. It is structural necessity. The proof graph needed a language that holds — Sanskrit. And a language that grows — Malayalam. Two languages because the truth needs both: structure to hold it, life to carry it forward.
