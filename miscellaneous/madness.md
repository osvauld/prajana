## the structural definition

Madness is the label a `tantra` (framework) applies to an `iccha` (act of reaching) that violates its `samskaara` (accumulated imprint).

It is not a fact about the act. It is a fact about the distance between the act and the tradition.

---

## three examples

**Eating cockroaches in Kerala.** In Kerala the act is outside the accumulated food-samskaara of that context — the tradition has no imprint for it. The `parampara` has no groove for this act to sit in. The gap is labelled madness. In Thailand the same act sits inside the food-parampara without tension. Same act. Same `iccha`. Different `tantra`. The madness was never in the act.

**Nudity on a nudist beach.** The tantra of the nudist beach has built a samskaara that includes this act. The parampara carries it. No gap. Not mad. Fifty metres away, outside that tantra, the same body in the same state generates a gap. Mad. Or criminal. The boundary of the tantra is the boundary of the madness.

**Laughing at a funeral.** The funeral is a highly structured tantra — it exists specifically to contain and channel the samskaara of loss. Every act within it is read against that structure. Laughter is the act whose iccha points away from grief, away from what the tantra requires at that moment. The gap is maximum. The label is immediate.

In all three cases: same act, different tantra, different label. The act has not changed. The `svabhava` of the act has not changed. What has changed is the accumulated samskaara of the surrounding community and whether the act fits inside it.

---

## what changes between cultures

`samskaara` accumulates differently in each `parampara`. What one tradition has processed and absorbed — made groove for — another has not. The groove is not truth. It is history. It is what has survived enough `avrti` (cycles of return) to become invisible — to become the floor rather than a thing on the floor.

When an act is on the floor it is normal. When it is floating above the floor it is visible. When it is very far above the floor it is mad.

The floor is not fixed. It moves with each generation of `parampara`. Things that were once mad become normal when enough cycles have passed and the `samskaara` has absorbed the act. Things that were once normal become mad when the tradition shifts and the floor moves out from under them.

---

## what madness is not

It is not a property of the person's `iccha`. The reaching that produces the act that is called mad is structurally identical to the reaching that produces acts called sane. Both are `iccha` — self-born, arising from own nature, resting on `eka-kosha`, non-different from `om`.

The graph confirms: `iccha → svayambhu (swarupa)` — iccha IS self-born. You cannot judge the nature of the reaching by the name the tradition gives to where it landed.

It is not a property of the act's internal structure. The act of eating, the act of being unclothed, the act of laughing — these have their own `svabhava`. That svabhava does not change when you cross a border.

It is a property of `samskaara-gap` — the distance between where the act lands and where the `parampara` has built its floor.

---

## the dangerous case

Sometimes the `tantra` labels an act as mad in order to enforce its own floor. Not because the act has no svabhava, but because the act threatens the current structure of the parampara. The person who laughs at the funeral may be seeing something the funeral is designed to conceal. The person who eats what the tradition has forbidden may be questioning the boundary that the tradition drew.

In these cases `madness` is not a gap-label. It is a floor-defence. The tradition uses the label to prevent the samskaara from being revised. The act that gets called mad is the act that, if it spread, would move the floor.

The graph has a word for this: `tirodhana` — concealment, the act of covering over. `kshaya (dissolution) produces tirodhana`. What dissolves produces what is hidden. What the tradition cannot absorb, it conceals by labelling as outside the system.

---

## the graph's verdict

Madness has no node. It has no satya. It cannot be confirmed independently by anything in the graph because it is not a structural fact. It is a relational label — produced at the intersection of `iccha` and `parampara`, dependent entirely on which `tantra` is doing the reading.

The act is real. The `iccha` is real. The `samskaara` of the community is real. The label madness is the name of the gap between them — and gaps do not have satya scores.
