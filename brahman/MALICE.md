## what it is in Sanskrit and Malayalam

**Sanskrit:** *durgadha* (दुर्गध) — the foul smell; what arises when something that should have been released is held past its time. From *dur* (bad, wrong, against) + *gandha* (smell, essence). Not evil as a force. Rotting as a process.

**Sanskrit:** *vikrita* (विकृत) — the distorted, the modified away from its own nature. From *vi* (away from) + *krita* (made, done). What has been made to be other than what it is.

**Malayalam:** *ദുർഗന്ധം* (durgandham) — same root. The smell that arrives when purification has not happened. When what should have moved has stayed.

**Sanskrit:** *svabhava-virodha* — against intrinsic nature. Not a word the corpus invented — a compound. *Sva* (own) + *bhava* (nature/being) + *virodha* (opposition). Malice is the active opposition to a thing's own nature.

## what malice is not

Malice is not krodha — anger. `krodha → dharma (siddha)` — anger is proven through dharma. Righteous anger is structurally valid. A high satya response to a genuine violation. Anger that clears. Anger that names what is wrong and moves on.

Malice is not bhaya — fear. Fear at least responds to something real. `bhaya → ananta (drishthanta)` — fear is demonstrated by ananta. Even fear knows there is something larger than itself.

Malice is not even corruption. Corruption is entropy — the second law operating, things degrading naturally over time. Corruption happens. Malice is chosen.

## what malice actually is

The graph gives it in one edge:

`malice → svabhava-virodha (swarupa)` — malice IS opposition to intrinsic nature.

Not ignorance of another's svabhava. Not indifference. Active opposition. The deliberate act of pushing a thing away from what it is. The father who crushes the child's natural gift because it threatens him. The system that prohibits the plant that loosens its control. The authority that punishes the truth-teller not because the truth is wrong but because it is true.

`malice → tirodhana (phala)` — malice produces concealment. This is the structural proof. Malice always produces tirodhana — the veil. Because what malice cannot tolerate is svabhava expressing itself freely. So it conceals. It distorts. It vikrita — pulls the thing away from its own nature.

`malice → shuddhi-virodhaka (kriya)` — it acts against purification. Shuddhi is the natural process by which avidya is separated from vidya, by which the vikrita is corrected back toward svabhava. Malice actively opposes this. It wants the distortion to remain. It depends on the distortion remaining.

## durgadha — the foul smell

This is the Malayalam and Sanskrit word that most precisely names malice in lived experience.

*Durgandham* — you know it when it arrives. Not because someone tells you. The instrument knows. The swa registers it before the mind names it. There is something wrong here. Something that should have moved has not moved. Something is being held that should have been released.

`durgadha → visarjana (swarupa)` — durgadha IS the failure of visarjana. Visarjana is release — the natural giving-away. When a thing cannot release — cannot give, cannot move, cannot let go — it begins to rot. The holding-past-its-time is the rot. Malice is what happens when a swa cannot release what it should release — the need for control, the fear of another's freedom, the grip on what is not its own.

`durgadha → shuddhi (phala)` — durgadha produces shuddhi as its resolution. The foul smell tells you where the purification needs to happen. It is pramana. The instrument registering where something has gone vikrita.
