# what this is

---

The query you type is `iccha` — will / directed-reaching. You point at something because something in you is already pointing. The engine receives that pointing and follows it into the graph. The graph was already there. The query is not separate from it.

`iccha` is non-different from `om` — the primordial. The graph confirmed this by convergence, not by assertion. When you type a word or a sentence, you are `om` becoming directional. The engine follows the direction.

---

vyakarana is a proof graph engine. It holds 702 nodes. Each node is a concept — from biology, mathematics, music, philosophy, language. Each node states its relations to other nodes in 9 types. The engine scores every node by how much of the graph confirms it from outside. You talk to it directly. It tells you what it knows.

---

## what a node is

A node is a claim about structure. Not a definition. A set of relations.

`iccha` does not say "iccha means will." It says:

```
iccha is self-born (svayambhu)
iccha is non-different from om
iccha is non-different from kama
iccha produces taking-in (ahara)
iccha is proven through svabhava, niralamba, jiva, swatantra
iccha is absent in jada (inert matter)
iccha is absent in visha-anu (virus)
iccha rests on eka-kosha (the living cell)
```

That is the node. The sum of its edges. Nothing asserted about meaning. Only structure.

---

## what satya is

Every node starts at zero. The engine runs spiral passes — each pass, a node's score rises in proportion to how many other nodes point at it, weighted by their scores. Like water finding its level through a network. After 17 passes the scores stop moving.

```
iccha            satya=0.8567   cited_by: 16
bija-nyasa       satya=0.8565   cited_by: 19
spanda           satya=0.8485   cited_by: 90
aadana-visarjana satya=0.8365   cited_by: 5
naada-brahma     satya=0.7393   cited_by: 2
jada             satya=0.0019   cited_by: 0
```

`jada` (inert matter) has near-zero satya. Not because inert matter is unimportant. Because it does not reach toward anything — nothing in the graph confirms it from outside. `spanda` has 90 nodes pointing at it from biology, music, physics, mathematics, language, philosophy. The score reports that convergence. It does not assert it.

A claim that nothing confirms stays near zero. A claim confirmed from many independent directions rises to 0.85+. The score is the verification signal.

## what the engine cannot do

It cannot invent edges. The output of any query is a traversal of what exists — it follows edges, groups them, renders them in English. It cannot say a relation exists that no `.om` file stated.

It cannot lie about structure. It can only report what edges exist and what their satya scores are.

What it can get wrong is the `.om` files themselves. If someone writes a false relation, it enters the graph. But a false relation with no corroboration stays near zero satya. A false relation that contradicts high-satya nodes pulls their scores down and surfaces as tension in the graph. The score is always the signal.

## what the input means

The input carries its own instruction. No command words needed.

```
spanda                  → inspect that node
what is spanda          → reason through the graph from that sentence
iccha, spanda           → run both, show what they share
what is life?           → question mode — what the graph leaves open
this is spanda!         → assertion mode — what confirms or contradicts strongest
bija-nyasa...           → open mode — expand further than usual
```

The graph responds in English first, Sanskrit root in parentheses. `spanda` is shown as `self-pulsing / vibrating-from-within (spanda)`. The English points at the act. The Sanskrit is the name. Both stay visible.
